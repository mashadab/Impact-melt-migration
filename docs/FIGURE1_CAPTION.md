**Fig. 1 | Permeability couples melt migration and ice deformation on Europa.**

**a,** Europa surface imagery and an inset showing part of the rim and interior of Mannann’an crater provide the geological context for impact-generated melt within an ice shell. The schematic section contains the simulated initial melt body above an underlying ocean. Dashed leaders connect this body to an enlarged initial condition, showing melt fraction, \(\phi\), in blue and ice temperature in grey. The planetary section is schematic and is not drawn to scale; the crater inset has an approximate 5 km scale bar.

**b,c,** Melt-fraction evolution for low (\(k_c=1.85\times10^{-16}\,\mathrm{m^2}\); **b**) and high (\(k_c=1.85\times10^{-8}\,\mathrm{m^2}\); **c**) permeability coefficients, starting from identical initial conditions. Higher permeability accelerates downward melt redistribution. Times are indicated separately for each calculation; corresponding rows represent different elapsed times. All evolution snapshots share a common spatial scale and colour mapping, with vertical views cropped to retain the complete melt bodies. Dashed horizontal lines mark the model shell base at 10 km depth. An initially temperate layer beneath this boundary provides a closed receiving region; solid bottom lines mark the lower model boundary.

**d,** Coupled liquid transport and ice deformation in the initial flow solutions. The left and right halves show the low- and high-permeability calculations, respectively. Black arrows indicate relative liquid volume flux, \(\mathbf q=\phi(\mathbf v_\ell-\mathbf v_i)\), on a common scale. Colours show ice-velocity divergence, \(C=\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf v_i\), with blue indicating compaction and orange dilation. For a source-free, incompressible mixture, \(C=-\nabla\!\cdot\mathbf q\): convergent relative liquid flux accompanies ice dilation. The deformation contribution to melt-fraction evolution is \((1-\phi)C\), linking dilation to increased melt fraction and local permeability through \(k=k_c\phi^3\).

Axisymmetric fields are reflected about the central axis and displayed at their native 200 m radial × 100 m vertical resolution. In **d**, colours and arrows are restricted to \(10^{-4}\leq\phi\leq0.9\); liquid-rich cells are grey.
